## 1. Premise / Central Mystery

Dark Plains is the coastal theater left after an eclipse war that taught living roads how to forget. The old king-mages did not merely lose a crown; they taught the coast a worse trick: that a **name** can be scraped from a ledger, drowned under a pier, sung into steel, or reflected into glass until the person still walking no longer owns the memory of who they were.

What broke the world was not a single clean apocalypse but a **theft of continuity**. Candle-law once kept saints, tolls, and oaths honest by naming them aloud. King-magic once let a commander spend mana like a monarch spends decree. Somewhere between the last honest synod and the first mirrored road, those powers stopped answering the living and began answering whoever could forge a prettier record.

The player arrives as a new commander with almost nothing: steel, a muster-hungry road, and the right to raise a **banner**. The central mystery is not “who is the final boss,” but:

> Who still owns the coast’s names—and what happens when a living banner refuses to become someone else’s ink, hymn, or reflection?

Every chapter answers a different instrument of that theft: toll ledgers (Black Pike), drowned tithes (Moonwell), iron hymns (Broken Crown), glass writs (Glass Road), candle keys (Candle Key), and finally a court that fields the player’s own victories as enemy armies (Lady in Glass).

## 2. Tone

Dark Plains is **gothic pirate coast meets funeral monarchy**. Salt fog presses Harborview’s walls. Bells answer bells that should be dead. Coin smells of black amber. Pilgrims walk ash roads carrying vows that burn like oil. Commanders are pirates of legitimacy: they seize routes, recruit the wounded, and bargain with saints who keep worse books than smugglers.

Keep these flavor anchors:

- **Gothic pirate:** harbors, toll camps, forged writs, drowned cargo, banners as black pennons.
- **King-magic:** the commander as living king on the field; skipped orders stacking power; crowns that teach by breaking.
- **Drowned names:** ledgers washed blank; people still walking their own erasures.
- **Glass / mirrors:** roads that learn formations; reflections sent home wearing a commander face.
- **Candles as law:** black candles holding saints and stolen recruits; candle-keys that open synod locks; hospitaller lanterns that are mercy with a jurisdiction.

Dialogue should sound measured, road-worn, and slightly liturgical—never quippy fantasy. Humor, when present, is dry as salt on a pike.

## 3. Cosmology: Three Powers That Share One Coast

### King-magic

King-magic is the battlefield sovereignty of a living commander. When the king falls, the battle is lost—not because soldiers are cowards, but because the army’s permission to be an army dies with the banner-bearer. Skipping a command to let mana stack is not only a combat rule: it is the old royal habit of **withholding decree until the road kneels**.

King-magic’s sin is appetite. Emperors like Grave-Aurel prove that a crown can outlive mercy. Ash Hermit Yara teaches restraint: east of Blackwater, king-magic answers faster than forgiveness.

### Candle-law

Candle-law is living jurisdiction: names written, bells rung, tithes counted, keys turned by hands that still bleed. Hospitallers, Star Lawkeepers, Bridge Bell Monks, and the Candle Compact all argue over *how* candle-law should bind, but they agree on one horror—**an unnamed soul can be conscripted by anyone with a flame**.

A black candle is not decoration. Mother Cindervein keeps saints (and failed commanders) alive inside wax and feeds each flame with a stolen name. Candle-key fragments are physical pieces of synod authority: whoever assembles them can open or close the court’s lock under living or mirrored law.

### The Mirror Court

The Mirror Court is doctrine made of reflections. It does not invent victories; it **archives** them, polishes them, and fields them again. Prior Moon Voss reads omens from broken water. Mirror Road Captains wear banners of commanders who already lost once. Lady Mirror-Veyra’s endgame is intimate: she breaks armies with reflections, then sends the surviving image home wearing the commander’s face.

Glass is not “illusion magic” alone. Glass writs can make Blackwater Gate answer a reflected signature. Glass roads teach the field your formations before you march. The court’s theology: a perfect record is holier than a living mistake.

### What a banner means

A **banner** is recognized jurisdiction on the road. Without it, a commander is a funeral shroud waiting for wind. Towns, tolls, and chronicles salute banners. The Black Pike Ledger and Glass Road Memorial exist because banners leave receipts.

### What a name means

A **name** is the unit candle-law can protect and the Mirror Court can steal. Erase a name from a Moonwell tithe and the person may still walk—but the river has begun recruiting them. Sing a name into iron and the hymn owns the formation. Reflect a name long enough and the living commander becomes a draft for someone else’s war.

## 4. History Timeline

### Mythic past — The Candle Roads

Saints, scholars, and oath-fires lit the coastal roads. Harborview’s candle court claims the old road was once continuous light from pier to inland shrine. Bells marked crossings; ledgers named every tithe so no drowned recruit could be claimed in secret. King-mages ruled with living banners, but still answered candle synods when locks needed turning.

### The Eclipse War — Continuity breaks

War-wizards and king-mages signed last lies at Eclipse Hold. An eclipse army broke the old roads—not only with siege, but by teaching ledgers how to forget. Somewhere in that war, a first war-wizard “broke the coast” (Harborview’s Starless Writ still hunts that sealed name). Crowns cracked. Synod keys fractured into candle-key fragments. Reflections learned to march.

### Aftermath — Parasites of the broken record

1. **Toll predation** (Black Pike): illegal road law that records banners as debt.
2. **Drowned recruitment** (Moonwell / Cindervein): erased names prepared as candle-soldiers.
3. **Iron succession** (Iron Choir / Grave-Aurel): sung names forged into funeral empire.
4. **Glass inheritance** (Mirror Court): victories rewritten as doctrine and enemy hosts.

These are not separate seasons of evil. They are the same wound wearing different instruments.

### Present — Season of the living banner

Harborview still posts hospitaller rounds. Riverside bells still try to govern crossings. Moonbridge fears silk under stone. Ashford keeps pilgrim ash quiet. Blackwater Gate trades writs that outlive signers. Eclipse Hold waits like a throat for whoever finishes the candle key. Into this, a new commander raises colors the road has not yet priced.

## 5. Geography of the Living Coast

Treat towns as linked organs of one body, not disconnected hubs.

### Harborview (western salt gate)

Fog harbor, fisher’s wharf, pilgrim fires, Salt Chapel, Candlewardens. Sister Avel greets new steel here; Marshal Orren keeps field law beyond the gates. Harborview sells gear, posts contracts, and decides whether a banner is cloth or protection. Its sin: it will recognize any banner that breaks a toll, even if that banner later feeds eastern hymns.

### The western road and Black Pike camps

Between Harborview and Riverside, illegal pikes lower across the road. Lieutenant Veyr’s toll camp is a ledger with spears. Breaking him does not “clear the west forever”; it teaches every later parasite that a living banner can still interrupt accounting—and it leaves a coin trail into Moonwell.

### Riverside (bell law and eastern opening)

Bridge Bell Monks, river shrines, vigil coffee estates, ancestor masks. Bells govern crossings and have begun answering Black Pike signals—proof that illegal tolls and lawful rings share a frequency. Scribe Naima works Star Law here: if ink moves when a drowned bell rings, the law is already under siege.

### Moonbridge / Moonwell Pier (the drowned road)

Moonwell Pier receives midnight cargo that is not cargo. Reflections know names before speech. Blackroot grows from interrupted bell sequences. Beneath Moonbridge, the Moonfen Brood Mother spins cathedral silk from drowned pilgrims’ last prayers. Sealing the Moonwell returns names to families—and opens the eastern rumor line that the Iron Choir has been waiting to sing into.

### Crossroads Camp and the ash approaches

Where cutters sell ledger pages and ash roads begin counting pilgrims. Side wars here matter: Tollroad Goblin Hexers still move drowned tithe pages after Cindervein falls.

### Ashford and the western ash road

Witherfield Farm, pilgrim hospice, cinder reliquaries, Ash Road Watch. Ash Hermit Yara keeps oath fire and teaches that east of Blackwater, king-magic answers faster than mercy. The Ash Road Revenant collects cinders of vows that never reached Eclipse Hold—false pilgrim omens that can turn hospice against living banners.

### Blackwater Gate (writs that outlive hands)

Checkpoint of living law versus forged glass. Gate Warden Sable’s ledgers still answer blood if the player arrives before mirror forgers buy every contract. East Road Camp listens for iron hymns. Granite Heights Quarry is where glass scouts and blue-oath wardens contest the approaches.

### Eclipse Hold (throat of the eclipse war)

Where king-mages signed last lies; where ossuaries open to reflected water; where synod bells crack calling key fragments home; where Lady Mirror-Veyra holds court. Shadowgrove nearby nests Scarlet Matron Yvane’s red-thread curses. The Hold is not only a final dungeon aesthetic—it is the coast’s memory palace, and every faction wants to edit a different wing.

## 7. Core Cast

### Sister Avel — Hospitaller of the Candle Road

**Wants:** proof that new steel will do ordinary duty before it claims glory.  
**Secret:** she has buried more “first banners” than she names; hospitaller rounds are triage for coast-wide failure.  
**Relationship:** opens the road to Orren; remains the west’s conscience when eastern crowns tempt.

### Marshal Orren — Harborview Field Marshal

**Wants:** western road recognition under living field law; soldiers who obey after moonrise.  
**Secret:** his first western campaign failed; the Black Pike Ledger’s “Failed Expedition” page is partly his. He uses the player to finish a war he already lost once.  
**Relationship:** mentor-examiner; guide of the Black Pike Ledger chronicle; distrusts paper formations.

### Scribe Naima — Star Lawkeeper

**Wants:** every stolen Moonwell name returned readable; funders of the rite named.  
**Secret:** she will keep a sealed copy if the player chooses the pragmatic path—Star Law hunts accounts as much as souls.  
**Relationship:** Moonwell chapter guide; counterpart to Kaito’s bells; later warns that mirror scouts ask for the same freed names.

### Brother Kaito — Bell Tower Monk

**Wants:** interrupted drowned sequences; living hands on candle keys; muted false synod rings.  
**Secret:** he knows which pauses in a bell sequence wake roots—and has let some sequences almost complete to learn the enemy.  
**Relationship:** bridges Moonwell, Candle Key, and Synod Bell side road; friend to bridge wards and dangerous knowledge.

### Ash Hermit Yara — Keeper of Oath Fire

**Wants:** commanders who skip a command and learn king-magic without becoming Grave-Aurel.  
**Secret:** oath fire can quiet false ash omens because it once fed on similar vows.  
**Relationship:** opens Broken Crown; guides Ash Pilgrim side arc; moral counterweight to iron succession.

### Veil Captain Serat — Eclipse March Scout

**Wants:** the Iron Choir Marshal silenced and the Mirror Court exposed before it wears western victories.  
**Secret:** veil scouting means she has already traded with mirrored rumor lines to map them.  
**Relationship:** Broken Crown midpoint guide; Glass Road opener; aims the player like a calibrated threat.

### Gate Warden Sable — Blackwater Quartermaster

**Wants:** living writs retained; forged mirror plates burned where ledgers can smell smoke.  
**Secret:** Blackwater has already sold borderline contracts; Sable’s purity is a repair job, not a virgin ledger.  
**Relationship:** Glass Road guide; Forged Writs side arc; practical ally of living law.

### Archivist Letho — Mirror Court Scribe / Glass Reliquary

**Wants:** the court act closed under living record—or at least a shard quiet enough to archive.  
**Secret:** every reflection in his reliquary is a confession someone refused aloud; he may have authored some refusals.  
**Relationship:** Lady in Glass guide; ambiguous—needs the player to refuse kneeling, which is also useful data for the court he flees.

### Antagonists (story bosses)

- **Lieutenant Veyr:** Black Pike tollmaster who weighs banners; first proof that the road can be owned by a ledger with spears.
- **Blackroot Vessel:** armored memory of drowned recruits finishing a bell with root nerves.
- **Mother Cindervein:** candle-fire priestess mothering stolen names into soldiers.
- **Iron Choir Marshal:** hymn captain who forges formations into steel before battle.
- **Emperor Grave-Aurel:** undead templar emperor waiting for a war worthy of waking fully; Broken Crown’s lesson about kingship.
- **Mirror Road Captain:** scout wearing lost commanders’ colors; teaches the court your next order.
- **Prior Moon Voss:** moonlit field priest opening sealed chambers with reflected water.
- **Ser Kael Duskplate:** blue-oath templar drowned in moonlit steel; cold vows guard key routes.
- **Key-Saint Ardan:** paladin of sealed gates; every lock he opens takes a year from the living.
- **Scarlet Matron Yvane:** blood-star witch knotting red thread around proud captains’ hearts.
- **Lady Mirror-Veyra:** illusion court wizard; last king-mage reflection; wants the player’s name by heart.

Side elites (Moonfen Brood Mother, Tollroad Goblin Hexer, Ash Road Revenant, Glass Writ Forger, Synod Bell Reclaimer) are not filler: they are the same theft happening on smaller roads.

## 8. The Player’s Role and Three Moral Tones

The player is a **new commander raising a banner**—not a chosen one, not a prophesied heir. Authority is earned by jobs the road can measure, battles that leave the commander standing, and seals that return names, writs, keys, and shards to living law.

### Three tones (lenses, not branching novels)

Already in `lib/storyDialogue.ts` as `oathbound` / `pragmatic` / `defiant`:

1. **Oathbound** — Guard names, swear road oaths, return every name to its family. Tone of candle-law fidelity.
2. **Pragmatic** — Trace coin, keep sealed copies, measure threats before announcing hunts. Tone of ledger warfare.
3. **Defiant** — Refuse borrowed colors, challenge wells and courts directly, burn tithe marks. Tone of living banner sovereignty.

These tones change follow-up lines, journal flavor, and how NPCs remember the commander. They must **not** lock main progression or spawn three separate campaigns. They are how the same spine is spoken.

## 9. Campaign Spine — One Story in Six Movements

Keep quest/chapter ids. Narratively treat the campaign as **six movements with cause→effect**, even where code still packs early bands under `chapter-1` / `chapter-2`.

### Movement I — The First Banner (levels ~1–5)

**Quest band:** `story-01-first-banner` through `story-08-banner-recognized`; boss Lieutenant Veyr.  
**Cause→effect:** Avel forces ordinary duty → Harborview gives the banner a road → Orren demands steel → Riverside opens → Veyr records the banner → breaking the toll creates western recognition **and** reveals Black Pike coin sitting beside erased Moonwell names.

Without Veyr’s broken pike, the west has no proof a living banner can interrupt illegal law. With it, Naima’s ledger mystery becomes urgent.

### Movement II — The Drowned Moonwell (levels ~6–10)

**Quest band:** `story-09-moonwell-summons` through `story-14-moonwell-sealed` (+ silk/tithe sides).  
**Cause→effect:** Pike coin funds erased tithes → pier cipher wakes a tongueless bell → Blackroot finishes the sequence → orders name Cindervein → defeating her returns names **and** frees the eastern rumor line the Iron Choir has been waiting to sing into.

Sides deepen the spine: cathedral silk would seal Moonbridge (no road home); goblin cutters still move ledger pages (the rite’s funding network survives the mother).

### Movement III — The Broken Crown (levels ~11–15) — deepen this band

**Quest band:** `story-11-eastward-march` … `story-15-broken-crown`.  
**Why it felt thin:** march → gate → marshal → emperor is correct structurally but under-motivated. Invented connective tissue that fits existing ids:

After Moonwell, returned names are not safe—they are **raw material**. The Iron Choir’s doctrine is that a freed name without a living banner’s protection can be sung into steel faster than Star Law can rebind it. Yara opens the east because oath fire smelled funeral empire on the wind the night Cindervein’s candles went dark. Blackwater Gate still answers living commanders **only while** the choir’s hymn has not finished learning western formations from Moonwell smoke.

The Marshal is midpoint because Grave-Aurel does not waste full waking on untested banners; the hymn must first prove the field already knows you. When the Marshal falls, the emperor stands alone—and offers the broken crown as temptation: *commanders who rule alone become funeral armor*.

**Effect into IV:** defeating Grave-Aurel does not end empire; it teaches the Mirror Court which victory to polish. Serat’s warning is literal: do not let a broken crown be your last reflection.

### Movement IV — The Glass Road (levels ~16–20)

**Quest band:** `story-16-mirror-council` … `story-20-glass-sealed` (+ ash/writ sides).  
**Cause→effect:** Court scouts wear the player’s old colors → Sable’s living writs are being bought → Mirror Road Captain teaches formations at the quarry → Voss opens ossuaries with reflected water → sealing the Glass Road creates the **Glass Road Memorial** archive and starts the candle-key race.

Ash Pilgrim side: false omens try to convert Ashford’s hospice into mirrored pilgrimage. Forged Writs side: counterfeit seals almost hang Blackwater.

### Movement V — The Candle Key (levels ~21–25)

**Quest band:** `story-21-quarry-ledger` … `story-25-key-sealed` (+ synod side).  
**Cause→effect:** With glass sealed, rival powers race fragments → Eclipse Hold’s recorded oaths are the prize → Kael guards cold blue routes → fragments gather → Ardan would lock the court against living banners at the cost of years → cracking his key keeps the lock under living hands **and** forces Lady Mirror-Veyra to field reflections openly.

Synod Bell side: cracked bells call fragments like frightened birds; mute them or the court finishes the summons first.

### Movement VI — The Lady in Glass (levels ~26–30)

**Quest band:** `story-26-deep-dungeon` … `story-30-court-sealed`.  
**Cause→effect:** The key turned; the court now fields armies built from the player’s archived victories → Letho’s writ is a reliquary of refused confessions → Yvane hunts banners that will not kneel → inspecting Veyra shows which old wins she stole → shattering her does not erase mirrors (mirrors remember longer than roads) but closes the act under living law and opens the endgame board.

## 10. Themes and Recurring Motifs

**Themes**

- Living law versus perfect records.
- Authority earned by banners, not bloodlines.
- The cost of naming, un-naming, and re-naming.
- Kingship as temptation (Grave-Aurel) versus king-magic as disciplined field craft (Yara).
- Every victory becomes someone else’s doctrine if not sealed.

**Motifs (reuse deliberately)**

- Candles / black candles / candle keys  
- Drowned ledgers and running ink  
- Glass roads, moon glass, mirrored writs  
- Broken crowns and funeral armor  
- Erased, sung, or reflected names  
- Bells that answer wrong towers  
- Pike, hymn, shard, key, thread as chapter “receipts”

## 11. What SP Roguelike Means vs MP Campaign

### Multiplayer campaign

One shared coast progressing through Movements I–VI. Boss receipts, town boards, chronicle renown (Black Pike Ledger, Glass Road Memorial), reputation, and deeds are **living continuity**. When Moonwell is sealed, NPC voice changes. When Glass Road seals, memorial ranks unlock. The story is a road other players also walk.

### Singleplayer roguelike

Narratively: the eclipse war never finished cleaning its echoes. Each run is an **omen-war**—the same unresolved thefts recurring as procedural pressure. Bosses and trophies are not “another timeline” excuses; they are unfinished wars pressing through fog. Permanent unlocks are the commander’s growing ability to recognize omens and refuse old mistakes.

Do not cross economies. Do share mythic vocabulary: banners, candles, glass, crowns.

## 12. Open Mysteries (Later Seasons — Do Not Resolve)

1. Who was the first war-wizard named in the Starless Writ, and did they break the coast by inventing mirrored law?
2. What exact bargain links Black Pike coin to Moonwell tithes beyond cipher pages?
3. Is Grave-Aurel a unique undead emperor or a **role** candle-law once crowned and king-magic refuses to bury?
4. How many living commanders already walk the roads as unnoticed reflections?
5. What does Archivist Letho owe the court he helps seal?
6. Can a candle key be remade whole without stealing years—and who funds that research?
7. What lies past band 30 on the endgame board when mirrors still remember?
8. Why do some bells open towers invisible from the riverbank?
9. Are oathbound / pragmatic / defiant tones accumulating a hidden coast-wide judgment?
10. What happens to returned Moonwell names when mirror scouts ask for them by the same ink?

## 13. Canon Rules Writers Must Not Break

1. **Do not rename or retire chapter/quest ids** for Movements I–VI; deepen text and connective tissue instead.
2. **Commander death loses the battle**—king-magic sovereignty stays literal.
3. **Tones flavor, they do not fork** the main spine into three novels.
4. **Names are sacred inventory**—erasure, hymn, and reflection are always violent acts, never cute magic.
5. **Candles are law and predation**—never reduce them to loot sparkles without jurisdiction.
6. **Glass copies living victories**; it does not invent triumphs from nothing.
7. **Broken Crown must teach kingship’s cost**, not only spawn an undead emperor.
8. **Sides are arteries of the spine**, not random errands; each returns a motif object (silk, ledger, cinder, writ, bell).
9. **SP and MP share myth, not progress ownership.**
10. **PvP stakes stay soft** per `docs/pvp-loss-policy.md`: rivalry and reports, not permanent army erasure. Permanent death risk belongs to NPC/story/dungeon opt-in.
11. **STATUS.md owns the ops queue**; this bible owns narrative SoT; do not silently reverse Filip’s park of P0/auth work inside story docs.
12. **Leave season mysteries open**—no sudden “it was all one villain wearing five hats” that deletes faction agendas.
